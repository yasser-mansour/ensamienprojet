# Listening fallback — if the official Unit 5 audio is unavailable

**Use this only if you do not have the official Express Publishing audio for Unit 5, Tasks 6–7, or the
classroom speakers fail on the day.** Do not present this as "the recording" — tell students plainly that
two presenters are reading the textbook conversation live.

This is optional supplementary material. It stages **only the dialogue that is actually printed in the
textbook** (Task 7's gapped conversation, reconstructed with its answers filled in from what the printed
page and language box support — see the presenter guide, Section 9, for the one gap — gap 5 — that is not
determinable from the printed page and must come from the official audio/answer key if you ever obtain it).

## Before you start

Say to the class:

> "We don't have the audio working today, so [Presenter A] and [Presenter B] are going to perform the
> engineers' conversation for you instead. Listen the same way you would to a recording."

Two presenters read the parts of **Engineer 1** and **Engineer 2**. Read at a natural pace — not rushed —
with a short pause after each line so the class can process it. Read it through **twice**: once for Task 6
(comprehension, no writing), once for Task 7 (gap-fill, writing allowed).

## The dialogue (textbook Task 7, gaps filled in)

> **Engineer 1:** I heard you're working on the new seatbelt designs.
>
> **Engineer 2:** Yes, I am. I think I can make them **safer than** our current models.
>
> **Engineer 1:** Really? How can you do that?
>
> **Engineer 2:** All I have to do is extend the **stopping distance**.
>
> **Engineer 1:** How? Are you going to make the seatbelts stretch more?
>
> **Engineer 2:** Yes, exactly. That way, the passenger's **kinetic energy** will be transferred to the
> **belt**.
>
> **Engineer 1:** That's a *[[great / brilliant / good — confirm the exact word from the official audio if
> you ever get it]]* idea.
>
> **Engineer 2:** Yeah, I think it is. We're **testing** it next week.

Delivery notes:

- **Engineer 1** is curious and slightly skeptical at first ("Really? How can you do that?"), then convinced.
- **Engineer 2** is confident and enthusiastic — this is their idea.
- Stress the six bracketed/bolded answer words clearly and slightly slow down on them — that's what
  students are listening for in Task 7.
- For gap 5, either presenter may simply say a natural, clearly positive two-word phrase (e.g. "great idea"
  as written above) — but tell students afterwards that this exact word should be confirmed against the
  official answer key before it goes into any graded assessment. Do not claim it as the textbook's confirmed
  wording.

## After the second read

Reveal the Task 6 multiple-choice answers and the Task 7 gap-fill answers exactly as given in the deck
(slides "Listening Round 2" and "Listening Round 3 — answers") and in the presenter guide, Section 9/13.

## If you regain the official audio later

Simply resume using it for future classes — nothing about the deck or this fallback changes any of the
textbook's own content; this file only supplies a live-voice substitute for the recording itself.
